03The problem

Everyone found out at the door.

The recipient, the delivery partner and the platform all learned the truth at the last possible moment, after the money was already spent.

What one fake COD order costs

Packing+Shipping out+Partner's trip+Shipping back=Cost with no sale

Don't block orders. Ask the right person before anything ships.

04The obvious fix

“Just add an OTP.”

That was my first idea. It sounds safe, until you try ordering something for a friend.

The scenario

Vanitha orders a ₹799 kurti on Flipkart for her friend Durga, using Durga's number. At checkout, an OTP goes to Durga.

  1. Vanitha checks out
  2. OTP goes to Durga
  3. Vanitha calls her for the code
  4. Durga's busy. Order fails.

It demands action now

An OTP expires in minutes. Miss it, and the order doesn't go through.

Extra work for honest buyers

The buyer has to chase the receiver for a code, in the middle of checkout.

Lost sales

Every failed code is a real order that never happens.

An OTP stops fake orders by stopping real ones too.

Business impact shown here is an assumption, to be tested.

05The solution

Your number. Your permission.

Nobody can log into my account without my OTP. So nobody should send a COD order to my number without my OK, but on my time, not in the middle of their checkout.

Not every COD order needs confirmation.

We only ask when the phone number in the delivery address is different from the number registered on the buyer's account. Orders to the buyer's own number go through exactly as they do today.

Same number

No confirmation. Ships as usual.

The delivery number matches the account number. The buyer is ordering for themselves.

Different number

The number's owner confirms.

The delivery number isn't the account's number. Its owner gets an SMS or WhatsApp with 24 hours to confirm.

Same kurti, with permission

Vanitha orders. Durga gets a message from the delivery service and confirms whenever she's free.

  1. Vanitha places the order
  2. Durga gets an SMS
  3. She confirms within 24 hours
  4. Kurti ships.

Try it as Durga.

One message, three outcomes.

  1. ConfirmThe order ships. Nothing else changes.
  2. CancelNothing is packed. Vanitha's app shows it as cancelled.
  3. Do nothingAfter 24 hours, it cancels itself.

Tap the link, then confirm or cancel with a reason

06Every phone

On a keypad phone? A voice call.

Keypad phones can't open links. So keypad phone users get an AI-assisted call instead, in their language.

  1. Press 1 to confirmThe order ships.
  2. Press 2 to cancelNothing is packed.
  3. Missed the call?An SMS follows with a number to call back within 24 hours. No reply, no order.

Answer and press 1 or 2, or reject the call

07Back to Veeramani

Hundreds of fake orders. None confirmed. None shipped.

With this design, every prank order to his number needs his OK. He simply ignores them, and each one cancels itself within 24 hours, before anything is packed.

  1. A prank order is placedOn someone else's account, with his number.
  2. He ignores the messageNo app to install, no support calls to make.
  3. It cancels itself in 24 hoursThe prankster's app just says “Cancelled”.

What the prankster sees in their app

08The loophole

What if the prankster uses their own number?

Then the numbers match, so no confirmation is needed. The order ships to Veeramani's address and he simply refuses it. He's fine. But the seller and the delivery partner still pay for the trip.

The edge case

A prankster orders a fridge on COD with their own number and Veeramani's address.

  1. Prankster's number on the order
  2. Numbers match. No check.
  3. Shipped to Veeramani
  4. Refused at the door. Trip wasted.

Make the refusal cost the buyer, not the partner.

  1. The partner marks it“The recipient never ordered this”, a reason of its own, separate from damaged or late.
  2. The buyer pays a refusal feeThe round-trip delivery cost, like the cancellation fees food apps charge. Never the full order value.
  3. Keep doing it, lose COD for a whileFirst three refusals: the fee each time. After three: the fee, and COD paused for 30 days.

The delivery partner's app

Collected before the next order

COD has no card on file, so the fee sits on the account until it's paid.

Fair to honest buyers

Only “never ordered this” counts, and every fee can be disputed.

10Reflection

The best safety feature is one honest people barely notice.

Ask, but don't rush

An OTP demands an answer now. A 24-hour window lets real people reply when they're free.

Only ask when it matters

Orders to your own number never see a check. Only borrowed numbers do.

The honest trade-off

A genuine order can lapse if nobody replies. A reminder before the deadline and a call-back number keep that rare.
