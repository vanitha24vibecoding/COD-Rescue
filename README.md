# COD Rescue

A trust and safety UX case study: stopping fake cash-on-delivery (COD) orders without slowing down honest customers.

In October 2026, a content creator from Madurai shared his number and address online, asking for Diwali sweets. Strangers used them to place hundreds of COD orders for TVs, fridges and phones in his name. This case study explores why that was possible and designs a fix.

## The idea in one line

If a COD order's delivery number is different from the buyer's account number, the owner of that number gets 24 hours to confirm. No reply means the order cancels itself, before anything is packed or shipped.

## What's inside

- The real incident and why everyone found out at the door
- Why an OTP at checkout was rejected
- The permission-based solution, by SMS or WhatsApp on smartphones and an AI voice call on keypad phones
- The loophole (a prankster using their own number) and a fair refusal fee
- Business impact and reflection
- Clickable phone prototypes for every flow
